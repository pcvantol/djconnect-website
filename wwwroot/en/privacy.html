DJConnect privacy statement

What stays on your device or in Home Assistant? What do the website, optional Apple notifications, VibeCast and support share with other services?

About this statement

This statement covers the public website and services associated with DJConnect. Peter van Tol is the data controller for the public website, the central notification service and support. Your Home Assistant installation is where you manage your profile, music connections and Ask DJ history. The central service has a separate role in the technical delivery of optional Apple notifications.

Why we process personal data

We process network and request data on the basis of our legitimate interest in keeping the website available and secure. On the same basis, we use technical delivery diagnostics to investigate notification service failures.

We rely on your consent to deliver optional Apple notifications when you turn them on. You can withdraw your consent by turning those notifications off in DJConnect.

We process support email and diagnostic data that you choose to share on the basis of our legitimate interest in answering your question and investigating the reported issue. We process the data needed to handle GDPR requests on the basis of our legal obligation.

Website, downloads and installation

The public website uses no tracking cookies. DJConnect can count clicks on certain download and installation links by day, destination and source to measure link usage. The counter stores totals without IP addresses, browser details or visitor IDs. After a redirect, the external destination, such as GitHub, receives your request under its own privacy policy.

Cloudflare receives network and request data, such as your IP address and the page requested, to deliver and protect the site. The site also asks browsers that support it to report network errors to Cloudflare. Hosting, CDN and security logs are separate from the click counter.

Pairing and using DJConnect

During pairing, your Home Assistant integration issues the Apple app a device credential that it uses to sign in to your installation. The app keeps it in protected app storage and removes it when you reset pairing. Temporary response audio plays through a temporary link. That link is kept out of persistent app storage and logs.

Home Assistant manages your DJConnect profile, preferences, music connections, provider credentials and any saved Ask DJ questions and answers. It uses them to run your session and, when storage is enabled, retrieve past conversations. A music provider can receive search or playback requests. Depending on your settings, your chosen voice and AI providers may receive text, relevant context or audio to understand a question, generate an answer or speak it aloud.

Data in Home Assistant follows your installation’s storage and deletion settings, including your own backups. Your chosen providers apply their own retention periods and terms to the data they receive.

Music DNA, deletion and private mode

Music DNA is optional. If you enable it, your installation uses listening data and preferences for personalisation. Ask DJ works without Music DNA. Clearing Music DNA, clearing Ask DJ history and deleting a DJConnect profile are separate actions in Home Assistant. Choose the action that matches what you want to remove.

Private mode prevents DJConnect from saving new Ask DJ history, Music DNA, personal recommendations or mood data for that session. It leaves previously saved data in place. During the request, Home Assistant can still send the necessary text, context or audio to the chosen provider. Private mode does not change that provider’s retention policy.

Optional Apple notifications

If you enable notifications, the central DJConnect service processes installation and device IDs, a derived user ID where needed, platform and app information, language, notification categories and the Apple push token for registration. It stores the token encrypted and uses it to reach the correct device through Apple Push Notification service (APNs).

Apple Inc. (United States) receives the push token and the notification it must deliver. An Ask DJ notification can contain a short alert and technical references for opening or syncing. Account passwords and the full Ask DJ conversation are excluded from that notification.

The central service records technical delivery information such as event type and time, counts of successful and failed delivery attempts, and Apple error codes. This helps diagnose failures and is separate from app diagnostics you share yourself. Turn off notifications in DJConnect on the relevant device. Use the separate channel switches where your app version offers them. Cloudflare also stores technical request logs for the central API.

When the central service processes an unregistration, it immediately removes the registration, Apple push token and its hash from the active database. An automatic cleanup is scheduled hourly. Its next successful run removes delivery counts, failure records and stale delivery statuses that are seven days old or older. Deleted data may be recoverable through Cloudflare D1 for up to 30 days after deletion.

VibeCast on a shared screen

When you cast a session to a supported screen using VibeCast, that screen receives a shared session view. Depending on the session, people nearby can see the current track, artist, album and artwork, plus playback status, what is next and a shared DJ contribution if available.

When casting, the receiver uses temporary session access to refresh the view from Home Assistant. You can also open the view using the address of your own Home Assistant installation in the screen’s browser. Session content comes from your installation and goes to the selected screen without passing through the central DJConnect API. Stop sharing through the cast control, close the browser view or end the DJ Session.

Support email and voluntary app diagnostics

If you email support, the people handling support and the mail service receive your email address, message and any attachments to handle your request. Cloudflare forwards privacy and support email to a personal Microsoft Hotmail account (Outlook.com). We keep messages and attachments in the privacy and support mailboxes for seven days. Microsoft says deleted messages generally remain in Deleted Items for about seven days and may remain in its systems for up to 30 days after the folder is emptied, unless Microsoft must keep them longer by law. You choose whether to send app diagnostics or post them in a GitHub issue. In a public issue, GitHub and other visitors can read the contents.

A diagnostic export can include the app version, language, connection mode, service availability and permission status. The Apple export filters out data including authorisation headers, device credentials, pairing codes, tokens and raw request contents. An anonymised export also filters out private Home Assistant addresses. Review the export before sharing it.

Recipients and international transfers

Cloudflare helps deliver and protect the website and hosts the central API. Apple delivers optional Apple notifications. Depending on your choices, a music, voice or AI provider, the support mail service or GitHub may also receive data. The location and retention of data at providers you choose depend on their terms and your settings.

The services involved may process personal data outside the European Economic Area. Cloudflare’s data processing terms provide for the EU–US Data Privacy Framework for transfers covered by it and European standard contractual clauses for other transfers to which those clauses apply. Apple Inc. may process push delivery data in the United States. We have not yet been able to verify which transfer safeguard applies when DJConnect sends the token and notification to Apple. Microsoft may process Hotmail data in the US and other countries and describes its use of the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and European standard contractual clauses. If you follow a link to GitHub or post an issue there, GitHub may process personal data in the US and other countries. GitHub describes its use of the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and European standard contractual clauses. Your rights and how to complain

Peter van Tol handles privacy requests. You can reach him at the contact address below. Where the GDPR applies, you can request access, correction, erasure, restriction or portability, and object to processing. If processing is based on consent, you can withdraw it. That does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing.

You can complain to the supervisory authority in the country where you usually live or work, or where the alleged infringement took place. In the Netherlands this is the Dutch Data Protection Authority.
